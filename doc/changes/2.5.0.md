# 2.5.0

- Add `keepMs` to the replay journal: a retention target expressed in milliseconds instead of an
  envelope count. A reconnect window is naturally stated in seconds, but `history` bounds entries,
  and its depth in wall-clock time follows the write rate — on a busy line the shipped default of
  1024 envelopes can be a few seconds. `history` stays the hard cap and still wins, so a burst
  cannot grow the journal without bound; with only `keepMs` set the count is unbounded and memory
  is rate x keepMs. Available on `replayListen`/`withReplayListen` and forwarded by
  `exposeStoreReplay`.
- Replace the journal's fixed-modulo ring with a head/tail window, because a ring indexed by
  `seq % history` cannot evict by age. Age eviction is lazy — applied on write and before every
  read — so a quiet line frees its journal without owning a timer, and the consumed prefix is
  compacted rather than growing an offset forever.
- Keep `since(seq)` answerable at the head regardless of retention. A consumer that is already
  up to date needs no journal at all, so a line quiet for longer than `keepMs` must not answer it
  with a keyframe. Behaviour with neither bound set, or with `history` alone, is unchanged.
- Add `replay.journalWindow()`: entries, oldest retained seq, its age, the configured bounds, and
  whether the count cap cut a `keepMs` target short. A retention window that cannot be observed
  cannot be trusted, and a cap that bites silently reads as a window that is working.
- Cover the retention matrix in `observe/replay-journal-retention.test.ts`: count-only behaviour
  unchanged, no journal without a bound, lazy age eviction, an unbounded in-window burst, the count
  cap winning and reporting itself, a quiet line not regressing into a keyframe, and a reconnect
  inside versus outside the window.
- Why this matters beyond memory: a short journal turns every reconnect into a full keyframe, and
  `experiments/slow-network-2026-08` measured that a keyframe large enough to outlast the heartbeat
  budget is itself what starves the ping and forces the next reconnect. Retention breaks that loop
  at its source rather than treating the symptom.
